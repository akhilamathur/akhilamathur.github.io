Senior Design Manager, YouTube Shopping 2022 – Present

YouTube Native Shopping Checkout

Personally designed the core flows as an IC from 2022–2025. The work proved out well enough to become a formal team charter — now leading a team of three designers building on the same foundation.

The problem

Viewers discover products constantly on YouTube — a creator's haul, a live event outfit, a recommended gadget — but purchase intent has always died at the moment of friction: leaving the app, hunting the product down elsewhere, losing the thread back to the video. The question wasn't "how do we add a buy button." It was how to meet a shopper exactly where their intent already lives, without breaking the moment they're in.

The approach

Rather than build one universal checkout, I designed for the shape of the purchase decision at each stage of intent.

Low-consideration

A creator's own merch, or a branded product with existing trust. The job of the design is to get out of the way.

High-consideration

Comparing options, checking fit, reading reviews. The job is to give room to research without leaving the video.

Moment-driven

A live event, right now. The job is to make buying feel as immediate as the moment itself.

What shipped

2023

In-app product research

Let viewers dig into product details, compare options, and find the best offer — without leaving the video. Eventually sunset in favor of a leaner flow once the data showed where the real value was: a deliberate call not to keep maintaining a standalone in-app product page just because it had shipped.

+15% same-session conversion
2023–24

Native checkout, launched in Korea with Cafe24

Built YouTube's first live-shopping channel — a world-first — then partnered with Korean commerce platform Cafe24 to give creators native, in-app storefronts. Reported by Reuters, Yonhap, and Korean trade press as a milestone for YouTube commerce globally.

2026

Quick buy for live events

Extended the same checkout pattern to real-time moments — buying tour merch directly from an artist's YouTube content while the event is still happening (Coachella × Lady Gaga).

The throughline

Three different products, one design principle: match the checkout to the shopper's actual state of mind, not a generic cart. The willingness to sunset a feature that had done its job — rather than defend it because it shipped — became a pattern I've carried into how I lead the team today.
