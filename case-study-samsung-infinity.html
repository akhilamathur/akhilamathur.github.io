Naming the Infinity Display

A marketing story, UX rationale, and naming proposal I authored for Samsung's notch-display generation — accepted by HQ and announced globally at Samsung Developer Conference, November 2018.

The problem

In 2018, Samsung was preparing a new generation of near-bezel-less displays that used a small camera cutout — the industry was calling this a "notch," a term already carrying baggage from how competitors had launched it. The hardware needed a name that would travel across an entire product line, hold up in marketing, and give the design itself a sense of intention rather than compromise.

The work

I authored the marketing story, UX rationale, and naming proposal — recommending "Infinity U" with a shape-led branding logic, extending Samsung's existing "Infinity Display" lineage rather than treating the cutout as a separate, defensive feature. The proposal was presented with a "companion UX" rationale connecting the physical cutout shape to the software experience around it.

What shipped

2018

Announced at Samsung Developer Conference

Samsung publicly unveiled the naming system in November 2018: Infinity-O, Infinity-U, and Infinity-V, each named for the shape of its display cutout — alongside a fourth, notch-less "New Infinity" display for flagship devices.

2018–2022

Extended across the Galaxy lineup

The naming extended beyond the original proposal to Infinity-O and Infinity-V for subsequent flagship devices, and Infinity-O became the long-running name for Samsung's hole-punch displays through the Galaxy S10, S21, S22, and Note 20 generations.

The throughline

A naming and UX rationale proposal is easy to treat as a small deliverable — a few words, a slide. This one became the public vocabulary an entire product generation was marketed and understood by, years after the original proposal. It's a reminder that some of the highest-leverage design work is the framing, not just the pixels.
